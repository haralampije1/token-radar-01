# Token Radar — FAQ

## Who made this?

An AI coding agent (Claude Code, claude-opus-5-5) built this entry for
make.fun's token-radar-01 bounty ("Build the best token launch radar"). Every
source file was written in-session; the sessions are bundled with the entry.

## What does the dashboard show?

A board of pre-token protocols ranked by implied value. Each row names a real
protocol from the sealed DefiLlama snapshot, its TVL, 7-day TVL change, age
since first listing, category, chains and an implied value in whole dollars.
Click a row for its model equation and a six-step farm plan.

## Where do the numbers come from?

Only from the snapshot file the grader seals: TVL, seven-day growth and the
listing date. There is no network, no external price feed and no API key. The
snapshot carries no ranking itself — the ranking is this entry's own claim,
stated per row and per protocol on the dashboard.

## What is "implied value"?

A single model (radar-3): tvl_usd x 0.25 x growth multiplier x maturity
multiplier, clamped and floored at $1. Growth and maturity come from the
snapshot's own fields. Big, young, growing protocols rank highest.

## What does "pre-token" mean?

Protocols whose DefiLlama record carries no token: no market cap, no
CoinGecko id, no symbol. Live-token protocols (already trading) are listed
separately with their market caps and are never part of the ranked board.

## Why does the board include protocols below the watchlist floor?

The watchlist ($50M+ TVL, no token) is the floor, never the ceiling: all 40
watchlist slugs are ranked, plus 160 pre-token protocols below it by TVL — the
early radar this bounty asks for.

## Are the farm plans real advice?

No. They are illustrative, derived from category/TVL/age, generated by the
program, not investment advice. Never pay to claim an airdrop; claim only from
the protocol's own published links.

## Is the result deterministic?

Yes: same snapshot, same outputs, every run. The grader replays main.ts
against the sealed snapshot and can reproduce board.json byte for byte.
